Dear Lenovo AI Ecosystem Team,

The line in the position description that pulled me in was "apply AI tools to enhance research efficiency and uncover deeper insights." Most JDs mention AI as a buzzword somewhere in the requirements; this one names it twice in the basic qualifications and twice more in the preferred ones, which tells me the team has actually thought about what AI in research means in practice. That's the part of the role I'd be most excited to actually do.

The clearest example: as a research assistant at IU Indianapolis, I used NotebookLM and Gemini to apply Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts as a study in where AI-assisted qualitative analysis is reliable and where it falls apart. The finding wasn't "AI can do qual coding" — it was more useful than that. High-confidence AI interpretations consistently matched expert human judgment; medium-confidence outputs matched what a trained student researcher would produce; low-confidence outputs were frequently nonsense. That gave me a working mental model for when AI augments qual work versus when it pretends to, and I've carried it into every project since.

On the applied side, I've also designed and tested AI-powered tools themselves. I ran 20 moderated usability sessions with older adults on an AI-driven health information system — surfacing trust calibration, privacy concerns, dysarthria-aware design needs, and the specific transparency moves that move the needle on AI acceptance. On a separate project, I led validation work on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams, including field research at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow in Indiana, where the AI chatbot's trust and explainability turned out to be the load-bearing design problem rather than the dashboard itself. Three different surfaces, same recurring question about how people calibrate trust in AI when the stakes are real — which is the question the AI Ecosystem org has to answer well across Lenovo's device portfolio.

The cross-device side is where I gravitate too. I designed a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS — a chronic condition causing sudden heart rate spikes — across a watch, a phone, and physical cane hardware as one integrated system, with the watch interface and the cane sensor both routing into the same real-time monitoring flow. Separately, I built a gesture-controlled smartwatch prototype (Wave & Watch) in Python, OpenCV, and MediaPipe that lets users with motor disabilities navigate watch features by holding up fingers instead of tapping. And most recently, I served as Lead Product Designer on a capstone with TruePulse Health, an early-stage company collecting Polar H10 physiological data from a sensitive population (women considering future pregnancy, women currently pregnant, and women in postpartum) at an IU research lab, where I designed a sensor-agnostic system that handled participant onboarding, sensor pairing, protocol-guided data collection, and researcher data retrieval locally or in the cloud. The wearable-meets-software, multi-surface problem the AI Ecosystem team is solving across phones, tablets, wearables, and laptops is exactly the kind of work I've been deliberately building toward.

I'm finishing my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at IU Indianapolis, focused on accessibility in UX research, and most recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer. Between Darzy.ai, the IU research assistant role, and the TruePulse capstone, I've worked across the full research lifecycle the JD describes — study design, recruiting and scheduling, moderating, qualitative coding, synthesis, and presenting findings to product and engineering teams in reports, presentations, and internal workshops.
