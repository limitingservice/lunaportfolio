The clearest analog in my work to the B2B senior-professional interview rhythm this role describes is field research I ran for an AI-assisted energy-monitoring dashboard project. Over a single field deployment at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow in Indiana, I conducted semi-structured interviews with a senior manufacturing operations manager (30+ years experience), an emerging IT worker transitioning into the sector, and a Dean of Engineering — three very different senior B2B respondents in back-to-back conversations. The brief was to understand onboarding, the role of an AI chatbot in their workflow, and what would actually have to change for them to trust the system in production. The interviews surfaced the finding that experienced operators wanted "70% global data, 30% local machine data" before they'd act on an AI recommendation — exactly the kind of nuanced, implementation-level insight that doesn't show up in a satisfaction score and only emerges if you probe.

On the data-quality / "moat" piece: in Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond's lab at IU Indianapolis I led an AI-assisted qualitative analysis study applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts using NotebookLM and Gemini. The headline finding wasn't "AI can do qualitative coding" — it was that confidence-band reliability mattered more than the output itself: high-confidence interpretations consistently matched expert human judgment, medium-confidence outputs matched what a trained student researcher would produce, and low-confidence outputs were frequently nonsense. The practical translation for IANS: the moat isn't the data alone; it's the kind of probed, structured, human-conducted interview that an LLM working from a transcript cannot fabricate. That is the work I'd want to be doing.

I've coded interview transcripts and observational data thematically across the IU labs, the Canvas LMS study, the Spotify usability study, and the older-adults AI-health-tool sessions (20 moderated sessions, transcript-level coding). The instinct to fix grammar but preserve the practitioner's authentic register is one I've built rep on. Qualtrics and SurveyMonkey are both familiar working tools for the structured-data-entry side.

Holding a pipeline of scheduled-and-pending interviews across multiple firmographic segments, tracking coverage gaps, and escalating when outreach stalls is the rhythm I'm already used to.
