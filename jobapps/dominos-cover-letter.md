Dear Domino's Team,

The line in the JD that grabbed me: "internal tools and administrative platforms that support operational and configuration-heavy workflows." That's an honest description of where the hardest enterprise UX work actually lives — the surfaces business users spend their entire day inside, where small interaction inefficiencies compound into hours lost across thousands of stores. The Team Lead, UX Design role on the Global Store Digital Products team sounds like it sits exactly in that operational-heavy space, and that's the space I keep gravitating toward.

Most of my recent work has been in enterprise-adjacent product surfaces. I led a mixed-methods study on Canvas LMS across eight user roles — students, faculty, administrators, and support staff — combining interviews, observational task analysis with time-on-task and click-count measurement, and heuristic evaluation. The study surfaced workflow inefficiencies that high task-completion rates were quietly hiding (the "users finish the task in 14 clicks instead of 4" pattern that's invisible from a conversion dashboard), and I translated those into specific recommendations on persistent navigation memory, mobile parity, faculty automation, and external-tool integration. I also led validation work on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams, including field research at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow that fed directly back into wireframe iteration. Both projects are role-aware, configuration-heavy admin surfaces — the closest analog I have to internal store digital products.

On the design + engineering integration side, I designed and built a browser-based AR virtual try-on for eyewear end-to-end in Figma, React, Three.js, and MediaPipe. Carrying a project from Figma into production code keeps me honest about which interactions feel great in a static frame but actually fall apart at 30 FPS on a mid-range phone, and what an engineering partner can realistically build versus what looks clever in a mockup. The full case study lives at fernandojluna.com. The "previous development experience preferred" line in the JD is real to me — the design rationale gets tighter when you've felt the implementation cost yourself.

Most recently, I served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer and leading a small team of interns through participant recruitment, user interviews, and analysis. I'm finishing my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at IU Indianapolis, focused on accessibility in UX research, and I've spent the last two-plus years as a Research Assistant across two HCI labs at IU. The honest gap: I haven't yet led a permanent team of designers inside a large company. What I bring instead is design + development range, an enterprise-UX research foundation, and a genuine instinct that the best operational-tools designers are the ones who respect the user's time the way a good engineer respects an API contract.

I'd love to talk through any of this in more detail. Thanks for considering me.

Best,
Fernando Luna
