Dear Prometric Team,

The line in the company description that pulled me in was the framing of Prometric as the "industry gatekeeper" for fair testing — ensuring that people legitimately earn the credentials they seek and that everyone who comes through your doors gets a fair experience. My Master's program at Indiana University Indianapolis is explicitly focused on accessibility in UX research and design, which is essentially the same instinct read from the other direction: making sure the system in front of someone respects their context, their state, and their right to a fair shot at completing what they came to do.

The closest analog in my portfolio to Prometric's user is a mixed-methods study I led on Canvas LMS across eight user roles — undergraduate and MBA students, faculty, program directors, advisors, librarians, and CTLA staff. The study combined structured interviews, observational task analysis with time-on-task and click-count measurement, heuristic evaluation, and a measurement plan to establish baseline performance for future redesigns. The most useful finding wasn't where Canvas was failing outright — it was where Canvas was technically succeeding while quietly burning users' time: high task-completion rates were masking workflows that took 14 clicks where they should have taken 4, mobile experiences that lagged badly enough that users abandoned the app, and faculty manual-entry burdens that compounded across thousands of hours. I translated those into specific design recommendations across persistent navigation memory, mobile parity, workflow simplification, and automation. That "the tool is technically working but it's not actually working for the user" pattern is exactly the assessment-platform UX problem Prometric's intern team has to solve.

Beyond that, I'm currently leading a graduate capstone as Lead Product Designer with TruePulse Health — an early-stage company collecting physiological data from a sensitive user group (women across the pregnancy continuum) at an IU research lab. I work directly with the TruePulse founders and with Dr. Gregory Lewis at the Kinsey Institute (IU Bloomington), coordinating across academic researchers, founders, and the participant pool. The participant-side design problem there carries directly into Prometric's space: the system has to be simple enough for someone under real physical or emotional stress to complete the protocol independently, and respectful enough not to add to that stress — which is a useful instinct for test-takers, who are very much in a high-stakes, high-stress moment when they come through Prometric's doors.

On the research side specifically, I've spent two-plus years as a Research Assistant across two HCI labs at IU Indianapolis, leading specific workstreams under each of my MS HCI co-directors. In Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond's lab (human-centered AI for older adults), I led the AI-assisted qualitative analysis thread — applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts with NotebookLM and Gemini — and ran 20 moderated usability sessions on an AI-driven health information tool. In Dr. Francesco Cafaro's lab (interactive data visualization, embodied cognition), I led participant recruitment across distinct cohorts, co-facilitated workshops, and ran field-study data collection using Kinect-based motion capture at the Indiana State Museum. That cross-stakeholder, cross-method research muscle is what I'd bring most directly to the user research side of Prometric's work.
