Dear AMETEK Rauland Team,

The line on the AMETEK Rauland site that caught me is the product mix itself — "Nurse Call Systems for the medical industry and school critical communication systems for the education K-12 market." Those two product lines are unusually direct matches for the two threads I've been working on most actively in graduate school: health-tech UX for vulnerable populations, and educational-platform research across multiple user roles. I rarely see both sit on the same job description.

On the healthcare side: I'm currently leading a graduate capstone as Lead Product Designer with TruePulse Health, an early-stage health company collecting physiological data from a sensitive user group — women across the pregnancy continuum — at an Indiana University research lab using a Polar H10 chest-strap sensor. I work directly with the TruePulse founders and Dr. Gregory Lewis at the Kinsey Institute (IU Bloomington), coordinating across academic researchers, founders, and the participant pool. Before the capstone, I designed a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS (a chronic condition that causes sudden heart rate spikes) — an integrated system spanning a watch, a phone, and physical cane hardware, with embedded sensors and an SOS button. The hardest design problem there was the episode-warning screen: the user might be dizzy, brain-fogged, or close to fainting, so the design had to handle legibility, large touch targets, and a calming tone all at once. That instinct — designing communication systems that work for people in physically or emotionally vulnerable states — is the muscle Rauland's nurse-call product line lives inside.

On the education side: I led a mixed-methods study on Canvas LMS across eight user roles (undergraduate and MBA students, faculty, program directors, advisors, librarians, CTLA staff), combining structured interviews, observational task analysis with time-on-task and click-count measurement, heuristic evaluation, and a measurement plan to establish baseline performance for future redesigns. The most useful finding wasn't where Canvas was failing outright — it was where Canvas was technically succeeding while quietly burning users' time: workflows that took 14 clicks where they should have taken 4, mobile experiences that lagged badly enough that users abandoned the app, and faculty manual-entry burdens that compounded across thousands of hours. I translated those into specific recommendations across persistent navigation memory, mobile parity, workflow simplification, and automation. Canvas isn't K-12 specifically, but the multi-stakeholder communication-platform problem maps cleanly: a system that has to serve students, teachers, administrators, and support staff at once without quietly costing any of them.

On the PM-adjacent experience side: I most recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer. I built the research operations from scratch — recruitment pipelines, interview protocols, synthesis templates, and shared insight repositories — and ran competitive landscape research, customer and partner interviews, user analysis, and product positioning work that fed directly into product and design decisions. The "interview customers and partners to uncover what really matters" and "analyze market + user data to influence product direction" responsibilities in the role description are the work I've already been doing — just under a research-lead title rather than a PM one.

On the integration side, which the JD specifically flags: a recent solo project of mine was a browser-based augmented reality virtual try-on for eyewear, built end-to-end in Figma, React, Three.js, and MediaPipe. The work covered competitive analysis, a 9-screen user journey, a 5-state interaction pattern (camera permission → face acquisition → tracking lock → live try-on → recovery on lost tracking), and a production-quality build that integrates MediaPipe's face-tracking AI capability into a browser-native experience. Working on integrations with external technology partners is the same shape of problem at a different scale — and it's the part of product work I most enjoy.
