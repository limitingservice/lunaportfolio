Dear VidaSana Wellness Team,

The line in the role description that did it for me was the framing of VidaSana as operating "at the intersection of health tech, UX design, and digital innovation." That's almost the literal description of what I've been working on for the last two years, and the part of UX I find most interesting because the design decisions have real stakes for the user's health and trust.

The most direct parallel to this role from my recent work is a graduate capstone I'm currently leading as Lead Product Designer with TruePulse Health, an early-stage health company collecting physiological data from a sensitive user group — women across the pregnancy continuum (women considering future pregnancy, women currently pregnant, and women in postpartum) — at an Indiana University research lab using a Polar H10 chest-strap sensor. The work has covered the full lifecycle: drafting the research protocol, selecting methods, shaping a brand identity, and designing a sensor-agnostic mobile system that handles participant onboarding, sensor pairing, protocol-guided data collection, and the researcher-side data retrieval interface (local or cloud). I work directly with the TruePulse founders and with Dr. Gregory Lewis at the Kinsey Institute (IU Bloomington) — coordinating across academic researchers, founders, and the participant pool, which is the same operating mode VidaSana's role is asking for in the "work closely with the CEO and a cross-functional startup team" framing.

Before the capstone, I served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer. I built the research operations from scratch — recruitment pipelines, interview protocols, synthesis templates, and shared insight repositories — because the company didn't have one yet, and I led a small team of interns through participant recruitment, user interviews, and analysis. The pattern of "translate user signal into design action without translation overhead, then ship the next iteration" is one I'd recognize quickly inside a fast-moving MVP build.

A few of the other health-and-mobile projects for range. I designed a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS — a chronic condition that causes sudden heart rate spikes — as one integrated system across a watch, a phone, and physical cane hardware. The episode-warning screen was the hardest part: the user might be dizzy, brain-fogged, or close to fainting, so I had to design for legibility, large touch targets, and a calming tone all at once. And as a Research Assistant at IU Indianapolis, I ran 20 moderated usability sessions with older adults on an AI-driven health information tool — surfacing trust calibration, privacy concerns, and the specific transparency moves that actually move the needle on AI acceptance for sensitive populations. Health-tech UX for users in vulnerable physical or emotional states is what I've been deliberately building toward.

On the research depth more broadly, I've spent two-plus years as a Research Assistant across two HCI labs at IU Indianapolis, leading specific workstreams under each of my MS HCI co-directors. In Dr. Francesco Cafaro's lab (interactive data visualization, embodied cognition), I led participant recruitment across distinct cohorts, co-facilitated workshops, and ran field-study data collection using Kinect-based motion capture at the Indiana State Museum. That cross-stakeholder, cross-method research muscle is what I'd bring most directly to the user-research and usability-testing side of VidaSana's MVP work.
