The line in the role overview about feeling "equally at home shaping big-picture workflow and strategy as you are prototyping detailed visual and interaction elements" is what caught my eye. That tension between system-level thinking and the small-but-load-bearing interaction details is where I do most of my best work, and the Lead UX Product Designer role on the DT-US Product Engineering team sits squarely in that space.

The clearest example of how I work end-to-end is a recent solo project: a browser-based AR virtual try-on for eyewear that I designed and built from product page through live AR. The design surface covered a 9-screen user journey across desktop and mobile, a 5-state interaction pattern (camera permission → face acquisition → tracking lock → live try-on → recovery on lost tracking) so users never see broken AR content, and a privacy-first consent flow tuned specifically to push permission acceptance. I shipped the visuals in Figma and then built it in React, Three.js, and MediaPipe — which kept me honest about which interaction details were elegant on paper but actually fell apart at 30 FPS on a mid-range phone. The full case study is on my portfolio at fernandojluna.com.

That's the project I'd point to first, but the broader range matters more for the role. I designed and prototyped a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS — a chronic condition causing rapid heart rate spikes — balancing interaction design, accessibility, and visual design across a watch, a phone, and physical hardware in one connected experience. I led a mixed-methods study on Canvas LMS across eight user roles that used heuristic evaluation alongside time-on-task and click-count measurement to surface workflow inefficiencies high task-completion rates were quietly hiding, then translated those into specific navigation, mobile-parity, and automation recommendations. And I led validation work on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams, including field research at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow that fed directly back into wireframe iteration.

I'm finishing my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at IU Indianapolis, focused on accessibility in UX research, which shapes how I think about responsive and adaptive design across devices from the ground up rather than as a retrofit. I also recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer to keep research close enough to design that the team could move on it without translation overhead.

I'd love to talk through any of this in more detail. Thanks for considering me.
