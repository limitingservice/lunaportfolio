Dear Perpay Team,

The mission is what made me write this letter. "Significantly improve the financial stability of everyday Americans" isn't the kind of sentence most product roles get to lead with, and the three-product story — Marketplace as the entry point, Perpay+ turning repayment history into credit-bureau visibility, the Credit Card removing the deposit barrier — is a rare example of a company actually engineering its way around the structural problems subprime consumers face rather than just marketing at them. As someone whose research interest in HCI is accessibility, I read that product strategy as accessibility work in the financial-systems sense. That's a domain I'd be excited to design inside.

The clearest example of how I work end-to-end is a recent solo project: a browser-based AR virtual try-on for eyewear I designed and built from PDP through live AR. The design surface covered a 9-screen user journey across desktop and mobile, a 5-state interaction pattern (camera permission → face acquisition → tracking lock → live try-on → recovery on lost tracking), and a privacy-first consent flow tuned to push permission acceptance from ~40% to ~85%. I shipped the visuals in Figma and then built the production version in React, Three.js, and MediaPipe. Going from mockup to live code is what taught me the most about micro-interactions — which transitions feel great in a static frame but actually fall apart at 30 FPS on a mid-range phone. The full case study is at fernandojluna.com.

The rest of my recent work spans the product-thinking side of the role. I designed and prototyped a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS — a chronic condition causing rapid heart rate spikes — across watch, phone, and physical hardware in one connected experience. I led a mixed-methods study on Canvas LMS across eight user roles combining interviews, observational task analysis, and heuristic evaluation, then translated findings into specific design recommendations. And I led validation work on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams, with field research feeding back into wireframe iteration.

AI in the design process is something I've been actively folding into my workflow. I've used NotebookLM and Gemini to apply theoretical frameworks across large qualitative datasets — 5,000+ Reddit posts in one study — and Cursor and Figma Make for early concepting and front-end work. The MediaPipe-powered AR project is a small example of designing around an AI capability rather than retrofitting one in.

I'm finishing my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at IU Indianapolis, focused on accessibility in UX research, and most recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer. The fast-moving startup pace and "drive work forward when things are ambiguous" mentality the JD calls out is the environment I've been working inside.

Two honest gaps: dedicated motion design isn't a daily craft for me yet — the motion in my work has lived in code (CSS transitions, Three.js animation in the AR project) rather than After Effects or Rive, and I'd want to ramp there. And I'd be relocating to Philadelphia from Indianapolis, which is part of why the in-person office model and relocation assistance in the JD landed as a real plus rather than a hurdle.

I'd love to talk through any of this in more detail. Thanks for considering me.

Best,
Fernando Luna
