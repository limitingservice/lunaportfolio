Dear xAI Team,

The closest project in my portfolio to the Growth UX work in this JD is also my most recent, and it was structured the way the role describes — every screen designed against a specific funnel drop-off. It's a browser-based AR virtual try-on for eyewear I designed and built end-to-end as a study in conversion-focused e-commerce UX. The whole project started from a single statistic — online eyewear has a 30–50% return rate driven primarily by "didn't look right on me" — and tracks the funnel logic from there: native AR apps lose ~88% of interested users between "Click Try On" and "Actually try on," browser-based AR keeps conversion 7× higher by skipping the app store detour, and pre-prompting for camera access lifts permission acceptance from ~40% to ~85%. The PDP, the consent flow, the face-acquisition state, and the live try-on with inline color switching were each designed against a specific drop-off point. The full case study is at fernandojluna.com.

That's the project I'd point to as the clearest match for "design intuitive, high-conversion journeys" and "translate insights into high-impact design decisions." The reasoning patterns the JD names — friction removal, progressive disclosure, loss aversion, pre-permission priming — are the exact moves I was making, even if the surface was AR rather than checkout. The same instinct translates directly to pricing pages, billing portals, and upgrade paths: figure out where the funnel is bleeding, design the smallest intervention that fixes it, and defend the decision against a metric.

The rest of my recent work sits on the research-into-design boundary. I led a mixed-methods study on Canvas LMS across eight user roles combining interviews, observational task analysis with time-on-task and click-count measurement, and heuristic evaluation — the kind of work that surfaces friction high task-completion rates quietly hide. I led validation on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams with field research feeding back into wireframe iteration. And I designed a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS across watch, phone, and physical hardware. Different surfaces, but the same muscle: rigorous qualitative + quantitative synthesis turned into design decisions that have to defend themselves.

I also ship my own front-end. The AR project went from Figma into React, Three.js, and MediaPipe — HTML, CSS, responsive behavior, and design tokens are working tools for me, not concepts. That keeps me honest about which interactions are elegant in a mockup but actually fall apart at 30 FPS on a mid-range phone, and it makes me a faster collaborator with engineering on experiment instrumentation and tracking pixels. I'm finishing my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at IU Indianapolis, focused on accessibility in UX research, and most recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer.
