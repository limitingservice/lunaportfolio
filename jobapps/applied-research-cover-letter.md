The line in the role description that did it for me is the framing of AI validation work: "frameworks evaluating quality, alignment, validity, fairness, traceability, and educational usefulness of AI-assisted outputs." That phrasing is unusually precise for the AI-in-research conversation most teams are still trying to scope — and it's exactly the question my own research has been pointed at for the last two years. The fact that Pearson is the team asking it — at the formative-through-summative scale, across millions of learners — is what made me decide to put my name forward despite the credential stretch on this role.

The clearest piece of evidence: as a Research Assistant in Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond's lab at Indiana University Indianapolis, I led an AI-assisted qualitative analysis study applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts using NotebookLM and Gemini. The finding wasn't "AI can do qualitative coding" — it was that confidence-band reliability matters more than the output itself. High-confidence AI interpretations consistently matched expert human judgment; medium-confidence outputs matched what a trained student researcher would produce; low-confidence outputs were frequently nonsense. That's a working framework for evaluating AI-assisted outputs against intended use — the same kind of validation question this role's AI work would ask, applied to formative assessment, learner modeling, and instructional guidance.

On the applied educational research side, I led a mixed-methods study on Canvas LMS across eight user roles (undergraduate and MBA students, faculty, program directors, advisors, librarians, CTLA staff). The study combined structured interviews, observational task analysis with time-on-task and click-count measurement, heuristic evaluation, and a measurement plan to establish baseline performance for future redesigns. The most useful finding wasn't where Canvas was failing outright — it was where Canvas was technically succeeding while quietly burning users' time: workflows that took 14 clicks where they should have taken 4, mobile experiences that lagged badly enough that users abandoned the app, and faculty manual-entry burdens that compounded across thousands of hours. I translated those into specific recommendations across persistent navigation memory, mobile parity, workflow simplification, and automation. The research-into-design-recommendation loop is the same one this role names for "translating research findings into actionable recommendations that inform product and custom design, AI-assisted workflows, reporting systems, learner guidance, and instructional experiences."
