Dear Cosm Team,

Two lines in the job description did it for me: "candor over polish" and "blur the lines of real and virtual." The first is the register most of my recent cover letters have already been written in, which I take as a good sign for fit. The second is the design problem I've actually been working on for the last year.

The most relevant project in my portfolio for this role is a browser-based augmented reality virtual try-on for eyewear that I designed and built end-to-end against an e-commerce conversion problem — online eyewear has a 30–50% return rate driven primarily by "didn't look right on me." The work spanned competitive analysis (Warby Parker's iOS-only native app, Ray-Ban's limited native app, Zenni Optical's 2D overlay), market research (the $11.4B AR retail market, the 25–40% return-rate reduction associated with AR features, the 7× conversion advantage for browser-based AR over native app downloads), interaction architecture (a 5-state machine across IDLE → PERMISSION → FIND_FACE → LOCK → TRACKING → LOST, plus a 9-screen user journey), and the production build in React, Three.js, and MediaPipe. That's the closest thing in my work to a Cosm-shaped project: immersive technology with a real revenue mechanism behind it, and a research-into-design-into-engineering pipeline I had to coordinate as a one-person team.

On the PM-adjacent experience side, I most recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer. I built the research operations from scratch — recruitment pipelines, interview protocols, synthesis templates, and shared insight repositories — and ran competitive landscape research, user analysis, and product positioning work that fed directly into design and product decisions. Right now I'm leading a capstone as Lead Product Designer with TruePulse Health, an early-stage health company collecting physiological data from a sensitive user group (women considering pregnancy, currently pregnant, and postpartum) at an Indiana University research lab using a Polar H10 chest-strap sensor. The work has covered the full product cycle: research protocol drafting, method selection, brand identity, sensor-agnostic system design (participant onboarding, sensor pairing, protocol-guided collection, researcher-side data retrieval), and stakeholder coordination across the company, the research lab, and the participant pool.

Alongside that, I've spent two-plus years as a Research Assistant across two HCI labs at Indiana University Indianapolis, leading specific workstreams under each of my MS HCI co-directors. In Dr. Francesco Cafaro's lab (interactive data visualization, museum field studies, embodied cognition), I led participant recruitment across very different cohorts — museum professionals via networks, LinkedIn, and academic publications, plus children and family visitors at the Indiana State Museum — co-facilitated virtual workshops over Zoom with narrative and design teams, curated sustainability datasets for the interactive visualization, and ran field-study data collection using Kinect-based motion capture to track how children physically engaged with the installation. In Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond's lab (human-centered AI for older adults), I led the AI-assisted qualitative analysis thread — applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts with NotebookLM and Gemini, and surfacing the finding that confidence-band reliability matters more than the output itself — and ran 20 moderated usability sessions with older adults on an AI-driven health information tool. Across both labs, the through-line has been turning theoretical questions into deliverable studies, owning specific parts of a study end-to-end without losing the through-line, and shipping the findings back into design conversations the labs could act on.
