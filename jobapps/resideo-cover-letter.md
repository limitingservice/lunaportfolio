Dear Resideo Team,

The line in the role description that did it for me is the role's "holistic end-to-end user experience approach that extends beyond the product and software use." Most of my recent work has lived exactly there — designing and researching systems where a physical sensor, an installation flow, an app, and a data backend all have to feel like one experience to the person actually using them. For a company whose products live in 150 million homes through Honeywell Home, First Alert, and Resideo, that is the part of the work I'd be most ready to step into.

The recent projects in my portfolio cluster around that intersection of sensor and software. I'm currently leading my graduate capstone as Lead Product Designer with TruePulse Health, an early-stage company collecting physiological data from women across the pregnancy continuum at an Indiana University research lab using a Polar H10 chest-strap sensor. The system I designed is sensor-agnostic and spans the entire pipeline: participant onboarding, sensor pairing, protocol-guided collection, and researcher-side data retrieval. Before that I designed a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS — a heart-rate sensor in the handle, an integrated SOS button, a paired watch, and a phone app, treated as one integrated system. I also built Wave & Watch, a gesture-controlled smartwatch prototype using OpenCV and MediaPipe hand tracking for users with motor disabilities. And I led validation research on an AI-assisted energy-monitoring dashboard for manufacturing teams — a different end user, but the same underlying pattern: real-time sensor data, control surfaces, and operators who have to trust what the system is telling them. The IoT-meets-UX framing the role implies isn't a stretch for me; it's the through-line.

On evaluative research for software and app experiences specifically: I've run moderated usability testing in several formats. The clearest example is a mixed-methods study I led on Canvas LMS workflows — five predefined tasks with time-on-task, click count, error-rate, and completion metrics, combined with structured interviews and heuristic evaluation across students, faculty, administrators, and support staff. I've also run 20 moderated usability sessions with older adults on an AI-driven health information tool, think-aloud usability sessions on the Spotify mobile app with System Usability Scale scoring, and iterative testing rounds on the TruePulse prototype with real women, including a currently pregnant participant. Hands-on testing of software and app prototypes is well inside my working day.

On the generative side and the artifacts the role names — personas, experience maps, service design blueprints — I've built those throughout grad school. For Spotify I led affinity mapping that surfaced six core problem areas and developed a primary persona grounded in real user behavior. For Threads I developed a persona around identity persistence and context collapse, grounded in Self-Presentation Theory. For POTS I mapped the full episode-response journey across the cane, the watch, and the phone app — a service blueprint in spirit if not in name. Affinity diagramming, empathy mapping, customer journey mapping, and thematic analysis are working tools, not new ones.

The role's "lens of UX, HCI, and social science" is the lens I trained inside. My MS in HCI focuses on accessible design and UX research; I work across two HCI labs at Indiana University Indianapolis — Dr. Francesco Cafaro on interactive data visualization, museum field studies, and embodied cognition, and Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond on human-centered AI for older adults. In Dr. Martin-Hammond's lab I led an AI-assisted qualitative analysis study applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts using NotebookLM and Gemini. Embodied cognition, conceptual metaphor, and human-factors framings show up in my actual work rather than just on a resume.

On the cross-functional and workshop side: I co-facilitated virtual workshops over Zoom with narrative and design teams in the museum lab, conducted field studies at the Indiana State Museum using Kinect-based motion capture, and built research operations from scratch at Darzy.ai — a remote-first startup — working directly with the founder and product designer in an agile cadence with no prior research infrastructure. On the capstone I coordinate across the TruePulse founders, an IU research lab, Dr. Gregory Lewis at the Kinsey Institute, and the participant pool. Translating research findings into clear, actionable insights for cross-functional partners is the working mode I'm most fluent in.

One honest note: I haven't worked with external research vendors yet — most of the studies I've owned have been recruited and run in-house — but participant recruitment, screener design, and protocol scoping are things I've done end-to-end, so working through a vendor would be a process change more than a skill change.

I'd love to talk through any of this in more detail. Thanks for considering me.
