Dear Dassault Systèmes SOLIDWORKS User Experience Team,

The two responsibilities in the role description that pulled me in are "explore and prototype AI-driven workflows that assist engineers in their design tasks" and "investigate AI tools to automate internal UX processes." Most teams treat artificial intelligence in design as one bullet point. This one builds it into the role on both sides — both for the user experience and for the team's own research operations — which tells me the SOLIDWORKS user experience team has actually thought about where artificial intelligence adds value and where it pretends to. That's the conversation I've been having through my own research for the last two years.

The clearest example: as a research assistant at Indiana University Indianapolis, I used NotebookLM and Gemini to apply Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts as a study in where artificial-intelligence-assisted qualitative analysis is reliable and where it breaks down. The finding wasn't "AI can do qualitative coding" — it was more useful than that. High-confidence AI interpretations consistently matched expert human judgment; medium-confidence outputs matched a trained student researcher; low-confidence outputs were frequently nonsense. That gave me a working mental model for when AI augments user experience research and when it just pretends to, which is exactly the kind of judgment the "automate internal UX processes" side of this role would ask me to apply.

On the applied side, I've also designed and researched AI-powered tools themselves. I ran 20 moderated usability sessions with older adults on an AI-driven health information system — surfacing trust calibration, privacy concerns, and the specific transparency moves that actually move the needle on user acceptance of AI. On a separate project, I led validation work on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams, including field research at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow in Indiana, where the AI chatbot's trust and explainability turned out to be the load-bearing design problem rather than the dashboard itself. Both projects sit close to the SOLIDWORKS opportunity — designing AI-driven workflows that domain experts (engineers, operators, clinicians) have to genuinely trust before they'll act on them.

The engineering-adjacent design angle is where I gravitate too. I ship my own front-end. A recent solo project — a browser-based augmented reality virtual try-on for eyewear — went from Figma into React, Three.js, and MediaPipe, including a 5-state interaction pattern, face-tracked 3D glasses rendering, and a privacy-first consent flow. The Three.js side gave me real, if early, familiarity with 3D scene graphs, camera transforms, geometry primitives, and the kind of spatial reasoning that computer-aided design users live inside. Separately, I built a gesture-controlled smartwatch prototype (Wave & Watch) in Python, OpenCV, and MediaPipe that uses 21-keypoint hand tracking to navigate watch features. Different technology stack, but the same instinct: design for users whose primary mental model is technical, and don't pretend the technical layer doesn't exist. The full case studies live at fernandojluna.com.

I'm finishing my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at Indiana University Indianapolis, focused on accessibility in user experience research, and most recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer. I've worked across the full user experience lifecycle the role description calls out — discovery, contextual inquiry, survey design, persona development, heuristic evaluation, usability testing, and reporting findings to multidisciplinary product, design, and engineering teams in written reports, presentations, and working sessions.
