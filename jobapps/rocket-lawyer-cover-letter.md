Dear Rocket Lawyer Team,

The line in the JD that made me apply was the one about understanding "how customers perceive, trust, and interact with AI-powered features." Most of my recent research has been pulling at that exact thread from different angles, and the Copilot AI workstream is the first role I've come across where the question I keep gravitating toward is also the central question of the team.

Three projects in particular sit close to it. At IU Indianapolis, I ran 20 usability sessions with older adults on an AI-powered health information tool, focused on trust calibration, privacy concerns, and where transparency moves the needle. On a separate project, I led validation work on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams — including field research at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow — and the AI chatbot's trust and explainability turned out to be the load-bearing design problem rather than the dashboard itself. And as a research assistant, I used NotebookLM and Gemini to apply Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts as a study in where AI-assisted qualitative analysis is reliable and where it falls apart. Three different surfaces, same recurring question about how people calibrate trust in AI when the stakes are real.

One more project worth flagging because it sits squarely on the Copilot question: I also worked on a conversational UI for legal assistance during a graduate course, aimed at common users looking for guidance on real situations like fighting an eviction or contesting a traffic stop. The hardest design problem in it was trust calibration — making sure the system signaled clearly that it wasn't a substitute for an actual attorney, while still earning user confidence by being transparent that responses were grounded in real legal articles, papers, and case literature rather than generic LLM output. That tension between "don't take this as legal advice" and "do trust the rigor behind it" is the same tension I'd expect a legal Copilot to live inside, and I've already spent a semester thinking carefully about how to design for it.

The broader research portfolio is intentionally varied. I've created and facilitated research workshops, supported field studies of published work at the Indiana State Museum with Kinect-based movement capture, and done qualitative coding grounded in theoretical frameworks like Conceptual Metaphor Theory. My work spans both qualitative and quantitative — qualitative is where I gravitate, but I'm comfortable bringing in time-on-task, click-count, and measurement planning when the question calls for it (a mixed-methods study on Canvas LMS across eight user roles is the clearest example). I've also worked across the full research lifecycle the JD describes — recruiting, scheduling, moderating, synthesizing, and presenting back to product and engineering — most recently as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer.

The mission also genuinely lands. "Everyone deserves access to affordable and simple legal services" reads as accessibility work in the structural sense — the same instinct that pulled me toward UX research with an accessibility focus in my Master's in the first place. Legal tech is a domain I don't have direct experience in yet, which is part of the appeal. The honest framing of the role — early-career, foundational, ownership growing over time — is also the right calibration for where I am: finishing the HCI Master's, off a startup research lead role, and looking for the right next environment to do AI-focused research at scale rather than at the seminar-paper level.

I'd love to talk through any of this in more detail. Thanks for considering me.
