Dear Summer Atlantic Capital Team,

The framing that pulled me in was the line drawing the boundary between consumer apps and "the infrastructure layer behind how modern capital gets allocated." Most design listings in fintech ask for a polished dashboard portfolio. This one asks for someone who treats the operating system itself as the design problem — the workflows, the data legibility, the human-AI interaction model under all of it. That's the layer I keep gravitating toward in my own work, even when the surface has been a different industry.

The closest analog in my recent portfolio is the AI-assisted energy dashboard I led validation on for manufacturing teams. The surface looked like dashboards — KPI tiles, anomaly detection, time-series charts — but the load-bearing design problem turned out not to be visualization at all. It was the AI chatbot layer: whether senior operations managers, IT, and academic engineers actually trusted the system enough to act on what it surfaced. I ran the field research at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow in Indiana, watched the trust calibration happen in real time, and fed that back into wireframe iteration. The pattern I'd carry directly into SAC Nexus is the same one that showed up there: in any institutional intelligence system, the visualization is necessary but not sufficient — the interaction model around how operators interrogate, override, and trust the AI is where the design either works or quietly fails.

Adjacent to that, I led an AI-assisted qualitative analysis study in Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond's lab at Indiana University Indianapolis, applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts using NotebookLM and Gemini. The finding wasn't "AI can do qualitative coding" — it was that confidence-band reliability was the actual product: high-confidence outputs consistently matched expert judgment, medium-confidence matched a trained student researcher, and low-confidence outputs were frequently nonsense. That gave me a working framework for when AI augments expert decision-making versus when it merely simulates it — which is the exact question any institutional AI-native interface has to answer well before it earns a place in an investment committee's workflow.

I'm also currently leading a graduate capstone as Lead Product Designer with TruePulse Health, an early-stage health company collecting physiological data from women across the pregnancy continuum at an IU research lab using a Polar H10 chest-strap sensor. The design problem isn't the sensor itself — it's the system around it: a sensor-agnostic architecture spanning participant onboarding, real-time physiological data capture, and researcher-side retrieval, with two distinct user roles (participants who need a frictionless wear-and-sync experience, researchers who need clean access to time-aligned signal) sharing one backbone. I coordinate directly with the TruePulse founders and Dr. Gregory Lewis at the Kinsey Institute (IU Bloomington), and the work has run the full lifecycle — research protocol drafting, thematic-coded interviews with industry professionals, user testing with real women including a currently pregnant participant, agentic AI testing for future features, brand identity, and the data-pipeline UX from sensor capture to researcher view. The translation to SAC Nexus is the multi-role architecture pattern: when distinct user types operate against the same underlying data layer, the design decisions live in the schema as much as in the screens.

On the design-and-build side, my recent work has been end-to-end. I designed and shipped a browser-based AR virtual try-on for eyewear — Figma into React, Three.js, and MediaPipe — with a 9-screen journey and a 5-state interaction pattern (camera permission → face acquisition → tracking lock → live try-on → recovery). The relevance to SAC isn't the AR; it's that I default to building the thing rather than handing off a static mockup, which keeps me honest about which dashboard interactions are elegant in Figma but actually break under real data density. HTML, CSS, design tokens, and frontend collaboration with engineering are working tools for me, not concepts. I'm finishing my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at IU Indianapolis, focused on accessibility in UX research, and most recently served as Lead Product Researcher at Darzy.ai, a remote-first startup, working directly alongside the founder and product designer — the same operating mode the SAC Nexus team appears to run in.

One honest gap: I haven't shipped inside a capital markets product specifically. My experience designing for institutional users has lived in manufacturing operations and clinical research, not portfolio managers or underwriters. What translates is the muscle — complex multi-role systems, AI trust calibration, data legibility under cognitive load — not the domain itself. I'd expect a short ramp on the financial vocabulary and a faster one on the design problems underneath it.
