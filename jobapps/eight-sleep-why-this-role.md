# Eight Sleep — Research Associate

**Application question:** *Why do you want this role?*

---

Eight Sleep's mission — using sensor-driven physiological data to give people materially better recovery, night after night — is the same mission I've been running inside in graduate school, at a different scale and against a different physiological problem. The Pod turns a bed into an instrument. My current capstone with TruePulse Health is doing the same thing for a chest-strap heart-rate sensor and a pregnancy population, at an Indiana University research lab. The design problem is identical: how do you collect high-quality physiological data from real humans living their real lives, without compromising rigor or the subject's actual experience?

Across my projects, the through-line is technology meeting bodies. I designed a smart cane and companion app for managing POTS — a chronic condition where the heart rate spikes on standing — for someone who would use it during real episodes. I built Wave & Watch, a gesture-controlled smartwatch prototype, for users with motor disabilities who can't reliably use small touch targets. And the TruePulse capstone — sensor-agnostic data collection from women across the pregnancy continuum, in coordination with Dr. Gregory Lewis at the Kinsey Institute — only works if every link in the chain (onboarding, sensor pairing, protocol-guided collection, researcher-side retrieval) holds up under research-grade scrutiny. The reason I want this role is that Eight Sleep takes that same user-first, body-first commitment and scales it to hundreds of thousands of nights — and at that scale the integrity of the underlying research is the one thing separating a real recovery product from a wellness gadget.

On research integrity specifically: my work has lived inside academic research labs at Indiana University Indianapolis for two-plus years — with Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond on human-centered AI for older adults, and Dr. Francesco Cafaro on interactive data visualization, museum field studies, and embodied cognition. Real human subjects, careful consent practices, IRB-adjacent protocols, and the discipline of not letting product convenience override participant welfare or data quality. The TruePulse capstone is the most direct version of that discipline I'd carry into Eight Sleep's product-validation and external-validation studies — sensitive subject group, lab affiliation, careful handling on the researcher-side retrieval, and the constant pressure to keep the science honest while the product timeline keeps moving.

What I've learned that matters most for this role is that findings only count if they hold up under later scrutiny. In Dr. Martin-Hammond's lab I led an AI-assisted qualitative analysis study applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts using NotebookLM and Gemini, and the finding I keep coming back to is that high-confidence interpretations consistently matched expert human judgment, while low-confidence outputs were frequently nonsense. The lesson — that the *quality band* of your data and analysis matters more than the volume — is the same lesson a sleep-fitness product needs to hold for athletes, clinicians, and skeptical buyers asking hard questions about what the Pod can actually claim. Capturing data that survives that level of scrutiny is the version of research I want to grow into here.

The "mamba mentality" framing isn't where my own writing usually lives, but the substance underneath — extra effort because the work actually matters, obsessing over the details because details are where claims live or die — is the working rhythm I've already chosen. This role asks for nights, weekends, in-home subject visits, hardware setup, and meticulous data quality. That isn't a deterrent — it's the kind of hands-on, high-stakes research I came into the field to do.
