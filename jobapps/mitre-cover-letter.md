On the human-machine teaming side, which the role description explicitly names: my research has been pointed at exactly the AI-trust-and-validation question that human-machine teaming systems have to solve. As a Research Assistant in Dr. Aquesha Martin-Hammond's lab at Indiana University Indianapolis, I led an AI-assisted qualitative analysis study applying Conceptual Metaphor Theory across 5,000+ Reddit posts using NotebookLM and Gemini. The finding wasn't "AI can do qualitative coding" — it was that confidence-band reliability mattered more than the output itself: high-confidence interpretations consistently matched expert human judgment; medium-confidence outputs matched what a trained student researcher would produce; low-confidence outputs were frequently nonsense. That gave me a working framework for when AI augments operator decision-making and when it just pretends to — which is the core question any human-machine teaming system has to answer well. I also ran 20 moderated usability sessions with older adults on an AI-driven health information tool, surfacing trust calibration and the specific transparency moves that move the needle on AI acceptance, and I led validation work on an AI-assisted energy dashboard for manufacturing teams, including field research at the Smart Manufacturing Roadshow in Indiana with senior operations managers, IT workers, and academic engineers.
